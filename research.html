Interactions are tricky at the best of times, in any linear or mixed model. Then adding genotypes (millions) to the term, it becomes infinitely more complicated. Way too complicated for me, so far. Looking at these interactions in the context of white cell composition is interesting, especially when beginning to consider disease status. Simply (for example), a person may see a greater (important) increase in gene X expression when harbouring a specific genotype, if they have more neutrophils. This can then be analysed with a disease status in a funny looking system, causal inference! So what is causing what... We know the following: increased expression of gene X is associated disease Y, genotype A/A increases gene X expression and the magnitude of expression change is dependent on neutrophil count. So we are looking at whether the disease causing these alterations? Or are the alterations causing the disease? A great question first made by Eleonora Porcu et al in 2016.
